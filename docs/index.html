User guide

Chess Pass

A chess opening trainer for Android. 52 openings for White and Black, ranked by how often club players at your rating play them, plus 15 traps. Every move has a plain English reason, you learn each line by playing it from memory, and spaced review brings it back just before you would forget it. Works offline, no account, no ads.

Get it on Google Play Read the guide

Quick start

  1. Install Chess Pass from Google Play. It runs on Android 10 or newer.
  2. Open Openings and pick the 3 openings you want free. Tap one to see its idea and its lines.
  3. Tap the key at the bottom of the opening, like Learn line 3, and play the line on the board from memory. The note on each move comes after your try.
  4. Come back to Today. It shows how many lines are due and how long they take. Tap Start review.
  5. Try Traps. Scholar's Mate, Fried Liver and Legal's Mate are free.

What is in the app

  • 52 openings for White and Black, most played first, ranked by club players at your rating from the Lichess open database (CC0).
  • A reason for every move, in simple English, shown after your try.
  • Drills you play on the board, with Hint and Start over.
  • Spaced review with gaps of 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month and 3 months, mixed across your openings.
  • Every off line move explained: a verdict, a score and a reason, with the engine numbers computed ahead of time, so it works offline.
  • 15 traps, to set or to dodge.
  • Share a game as PGN from any chess app to see where it left your line.
  • Backup to a file you pick. Progress stays on the phone and out of cloud backups.
  • No account, no ads, no tracking, and no internet permission.

Free and Pro

Free: any 3 openings you pick, with every line, plus 3 traps (Scholar's Mate, Fried Liver and Legal's Mate). It never grows past 3 openings, even when you have learned all 3. Pro is one optional purchase of 2.49 EUR, or 1.99 in countries with lower prices, paid once through Google Play: all 52 openings and all 15 traps. No subscription. Chess Pass promises no rating gain; how far you get depends on your practice.

Where the numbers come from

Opening names and codes come from the public domain lichess-org/chess-openings data set. How often each opening is played comes from the Lichess open database (CC0), counted ahead of time and shipped inside the app. Chess Pass is independent and is not made, endorsed or sponsored by Lichess. The notes on every move are written for Chess Pass. The move sounds are CC0 recordings from Freesound.
